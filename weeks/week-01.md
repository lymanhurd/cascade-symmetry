### **Lecture Overview: What Are Symmetries?**

**Core Content**

> 1. **Introduction & Motivation**
   * Class introduction and overview of course goals.
   * Concept definition: *Symmetry as a transformation that leaves a pattern visually unchanged.*
> 2. **Media Spotlight**
   * Short film: **Eames' "Symmetry" (1961)** to bridge intuitive observation and mathematical invariance via dynamic visual animations:
[![Eames' "Symmetry" (1961)](https://img.youtube.com/vi/OHA6Hcj7P8o/mqdefault.jpg)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OHA6Hcj7P8o)
> 3. **Nature, Art, & Architecture**
   * Biological and crystalline examples (bilateral symmetry, radial floral/marine life, 6-fold snowflakes).
   * Cultural and historical designs: Chinese patterns, Roman mosaics, quilts, and Islamic geometric ornamentations.
> 4. **Mathematical Tilings**
   * **Platonic Tilings:** Regular tilings using identical regular polygons (triangles, squares, hexagons).
   * **Archimedean Tilings:** Semi-regular tilings combining multiple regular polygons while keeping vertex configurations uniform.
> 5. **Interactive Exercises & Sorting Activities**
   * **Pattern Grouping Activity:**
     * Handout of pattern images.
     * Discussion on classification criteria: lattice structure (e.g., square grid) vs. symmetry type (reflective vs. purely rotational).
   * **Block Capital Alphabet Sorting:**
     * Sorting printed capital letters by symmetry.
> 6. **Non-Planar, Hyperbolic, & Scaling Symmetries**
   * **Spherical Symmetry:** Physical demonstration using Temari balls.
   * **Hyperbolic Tilings:** M.C. Escher's *Circle Limit* woodcuts.
   * **Fractals & Self-Similarity:** Brief overview of patterns that repeat across scales.
